The next EU AI Act phase begins August 2, 2026

Are Your AI Agents Ready for the EU AI Act?

From August 2, 2026, further EU AI Act requirements take effect, including Article 50 transparency obligations and stronger enforcement for general-purpose AI model providers. High-risk rules follow under the current EU timeline, with many stand-alone high-risk systems covered from December 2, 2027 and systems built into regulated products from August 2, 2028.

If your organization builds or deploys AI agents in employment, education, credit, essential services, critical infrastructure or regulated products, policies alone will not give reviewers the evidence they need. Your teams need to show how an agent behaved, how its outputs were measured and what happened when a production run failed.

Progress AI Observability records model calls, tool activity, retrieval steps, outputs, errors, latency, token use and evaluation results. Engineering, risk and compliance teams can inspect that evidence as part of their EU AI Act readiness work.

The short answer

How Can AI Observability Support EU AI Act Readiness?

AI observability can help teams keep records of agent activity, monitor output quality and performance, investigate failures and give authorized reviewers technical context.

These jobs relate to requirements in Articles 9, 12, 13, 14, 15 and 72 of the EU AI Act. Observability covers only part of the work. Legal classification, risk ownership, technical documentation, conformity assessment and human oversight procedures remain the responsibility of the provider or deployer.

Key dates

Which EU AI Act Dates Matter?

The Act applies in stages. The dates that matter depend on your system, its intended use and your role in the AI value chain.

  1. February 2, 2025
    Prohibited AI practices and AI literacy obligations began to apply.
  2. August 2, 2025
    Governance rules and obligations for general-purpose AI models began to apply.
  3. August 2, 2026 Next phase
    Article 50 transparency obligations apply, alongside wider provisions and stronger enforcement for general-purpose AI model providers.
  4. December 2, 2027
    Under the current EU timeline, rules apply to many stand-alone high-risk systems in areas such as employment, education, biometrics, critical infrastructure and migration.
  5. August 2, 2028
    Under the current EU timeline, rules apply to high-risk AI integrated into regulated products.

The European Commission may publish further guidance or adjust implementation details. Review the timeline again before publication and during future page updates.

Scope

Could Your AI Agent Fall Under High-Risk Rules?

The classification depends on the agent's intended purpose and deployment context. Two agents using the same model may face different obligations.

Teams outside these categories may still need better records, evaluation evidence and oversight for internal governance, customer reviews or sector requirements.

Examples may include AI used for

  • Recruitment, worker management or access to education
  • Credit decisions or access to essential services
  • Critical infrastructure and certain public-sector decisions
  • Products covered by EU safety legislation, including some machinery and medical devices
What the platform does

Four Jobs Observability Does for Readiness

Each capability maps to specific EU AI Act articles — with a clear line between what the platform provides and what your organization still owns.

01
Article 12 · Record-Keeping

Keep an Inspectable Record of Agent Activity

Article 12 requires high-risk AI systems to support automatic event logging over their lifetime. Those records should help teams trace system operation, identify situations that may create risk and support post-market monitoring.

Progress AI Observability records the execution path across:

  • Prompts and model calls
  • Tool calls, workflow steps and state changes
  • Retrieval activity and source context
  • Outputs, errors, latency, token use and estimated cost

When an agent produces a bad result, your team can inspect the run instead of trying to rebuild it across separate logs.

Available today AI agent tracing, prompt logging, tool and retrieval tracing, performance monitoring and OpenTelemetry ingestion.
02
Articles 9 & 15 · Risk Management & Performance

Measure Quality and Performance Against Defined Criteria

Article 9 treats risk management as an ongoing process across the AI system lifecycle. Article 15 covers accuracy, consistency and cybersecurity, with performance assessed against metrics that fit the system's intended purpose.

Progress AI Observability helps teams:

  • Run large language model (LLM)-as-a-judge evaluations on real traces
  • Score outputs against criteria such as relevance, usefulness, groundedness or policy alignment
  • Compare prompt or model changes against the same evaluation dataset
  • Review quality, errors, latency, token use and cost in the same workflow

Your team defines the criteria. Progress gives you a repeatable way to apply them and keep each score connected to the production activity behind the output.

Available today Production evaluations, evaluator scores, quality scorecards, evaluation datasets and side-by-side experiments.
03
Articles 9 & 72 · Ongoing & Post-Market Monitoring

Investigate Failures and Monitor Behavior After Release

Article 72 requires providers of high-risk AI systems to establish and document a post-market monitoring system. That system must collect and analyze relevant performance data throughout the system's lifetime.

A metric can tell you that behavior changed. An investigation still needs the context behind it. Progress connects the final output with the prompts, model calls, retrieved information, tools, errors and performance data that shaped the run. Teams can review:

  • A tool call that returned an incorrect or unexpected result
  • A retrieval step that supplied weak or irrelevant context
  • An agent loop that drove up latency, tokens or cost
  • A failed output alongside its evaluation scores and trace data

Production traces, historical evaluations and anomaly alerts can supply evidence for monitoring and review. Your organization still defines the formal monitoring plan, ownership model and reporting process.

Available today End-to-end traces, anomaly and threshold alerts, historical evaluations, cost analysis and token tracking.
04
Articles 13 & 14 · Transparency & Human Oversight

Give Reviewers Evidence They Can Use

Article 13 requires providers to give deployers information that helps them understand a system's capabilities, limitations and outputs. Article 14 requires high-risk systems to support effective oversight by people during use.

Progress AI Observability gives authorized reviewers a clearer record of:

  • The activity that led to an output
  • The models, tools and retrieved information involved
  • Evaluation results and known failure examples
  • Cost, latency, error and usage data linked to the run

Enterprise access controls and audit logs help organizations manage who can review operational data and keep a record of activity inside the platform. Progress does not decide who can approve, interrupt or override an AI system. Those responsibilities sit within the organization's governance and operating procedures.

Available today Trace-level review, evaluation scorecards, access controls, audit logs and enterprise deployment options.

Boundaries

What AI Observability Does Not Cover

Progress AI Observability can support selected technical parts of an EU AI Act readiness program. It does not:

  • Classify an AI system under the Act
  • Complete a conformity assessment
  • Create the provider's full technical documentation
  • Define the organization's human oversight or incident-reporting process

Those decisions require legal, security, product and operational ownership. Observability gives those teams inspectable evidence about what the AI system did.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Does AI Observability Make an Organization Compliant With the EU AI Act?

No. AI observability can provide runtime records, evaluation results and monitoring data that support parts of a readiness program. Compliance may also require legal classification, risk management, technical documentation, human oversight, conformity assessment and other organizational processes.

Does the EU AI Act Apply to Every AI Agent?

No. Obligations depend on the system's intended purpose, risk classification and the role of the organization providing or deploying it. Some transparency requirements apply outside the high-risk category.

Where Does Progress Host AI Observability Data?

Customer data is hosted in the European Union by default. U.S. hosting is available by request. Enterprise deployment options include on-premises hosting and approved residency configurations.

Can Progress Work With an Existing Observability Stack?

Yes. Progress AI Observability uses OpenTelemetry and is designed to work beside existing application performance monitoring tools. SDK support includes .NET, Python and TypeScript.
